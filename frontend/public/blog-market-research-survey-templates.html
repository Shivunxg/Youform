What Is a Market Research Survey?

A market research survey is a structured questionnaire used to collect data from a target audience — typically to understand customer needs, validate a product idea, evaluate demand, or study buying behaviour. Unlike informal interviews, a survey gives you quantifiable data you can analyze, compare, and act on.

Market research surveys are used by:

  • Startups validating a product idea before building
  • Product teams identifying features customers actually want
  • Marketing teams understanding who buys their product and why
  • Small businesses researching local demand or competitor positioning
  • Agencies conducting audience research on behalf of clients

4 Types of Market Research Survey Templates

Each type of market survey has a different purpose, a different audience, and a different question format. Using the wrong template for the wrong purpose is the most common market research mistake.

🎯

Market Research Survey Template #1: Audience Research

An audience research survey tells you who your customers are beyond their email address. It captures the context, motivations, and language your customers use — which directly feeds into your marketing, positioning, and product decisions.

Sample questions
1
What is your primary role or job title?
Multiple choice or Short text
2
What industry do you work in?
Dropdown
3
What is the biggest challenge you face with [topic] right now?
Long text · Open-ended
4
How are you currently solving this problem? What tools or methods are you using?
Long text · Open-ended
5
What would the ideal solution look like for you?
Long text · Open-ended
6
Where do you typically go to find information about [topic]? (Select all that apply)
Checkbox — Google, YouTube, Reddit, LinkedIn, Peer recommendations, Industry blogs
7
How much do you currently spend per month on tools related to [topic]?
Multiple choice — ranges

Open-ended questions (3, 4, 5) generate the most useful data for audience research. Read the verbatim responses carefully — the exact words customers use to describe their problem become your headline copy.

Market Research Survey Template #2: Product Validation

A product validation survey tests demand for something that doesn't exist yet. The goal is to find out whether people have the problem you think they have, whether they're actively looking for a solution, and whether they'd pay for yours.

Sample questions
1
How often do you experience [problem]?
Multiple choice — Daily / Weekly / Monthly / Rarely / Never
2
When [problem] occurs, what is the impact on you or your business?
Multiple choice — Critical / Significant / Minor / No impact
3
Have you tried to find a solution to [problem] in the past 6 months?
Yes / No + conditional follow-up
4
If a product could [solve problem], how likely would you be to try it?
1–5 scale
5
What would you expect to pay per month for this solution?
Multiple choice — price ranges
6
What's the main reason you would or wouldn't use this product?
Long text · Open-ended

The key signal in a product validation survey is questions 2 and 3 together. If the problem is minor and they haven't tried to solve it in the last 6 months, the market may not be ready — no matter how many people say they'd "definitely use" the product in question 4.

Market Research Survey Template #3: Competitor Analysis

A competitor analysis survey reveals what your target customers think of the alternatives they're already using — which is where most switching decisions start. Instead of guessing at your competitors' weaknesses, you can ask the people who've experienced them.

Sample questions
1
Which tools or services do you currently use for [category]? (Select all that apply)
Checkbox — competitor list + "Other"
2
How satisfied are you with your current solution?
1–10 scale
3
What do you like most about [competitor]?
Long text
4
What frustrates you most about [competitor]?
Long text
5
What would make you consider switching to a different solution?
Long text
6
What feature is missing from your current tool that you wish existed?
Long text

Questions 4 and 6 are the most valuable for positioning. The frustrations people name about competitors become your differentiation points. The missing features they describe become your product roadmap inputs.

Market Research Survey Template #4: Brand Awareness

A brand awareness survey measures how well your target market knows you — and what they associate with your brand when they do. Run this before and after a marketing campaign to measure impact.

Sample questions
1
When you think of [category], what brands come to mind? (Unaided recall)
Short text — don't suggest names here
2
Have you heard of [Brand Name]?
Yes / No + conditional follow-up
3
How would you describe [Brand Name] to someone who hasn't heard of it?
Long text · Shown to: those who said Yes to Q2
4
Which of the following words or phrases would you associate with [Brand Name]?
Checkbox — pre-set descriptors
5
Where have you seen or heard about [Brand Name]?
Checkbox — channels
Tip

For unaided recall (Q1), never pre-fill the text field with your brand name or hint at it. Any prompt contaminates the data. Let respondents type freely — you want to see if you're in their top-of-mind set without prompting.

Market Survey Format Best Practices

Keep it under 10 questions

A market research survey that takes more than 5–7 minutes to complete sees significantly higher drop-off. For most market research goals, 6–10 questions is optimal. If you need more data, run two shorter surveys rather than one long one.

Start with an easy question

Begin with a simple, non-threatening question (role, industry, product usage frequency). This builds momentum and filters out unqualified respondents early — before they reach the questions that matter.

Use a mix of question types

  • Multiple choice for quantifiable, comparable data
  • Rating scales (1–5 or 1–10) for measuring intensity or satisfaction
  • Open-ended text for capturing language and nuance
  • Checkbox for "select all that apply" questions

Surveys that are 100% multiple choice give you clean data but miss the texture. Surveys that are 100% open-ended are hard to analyze. Mix them.

Avoid leading questions

A leading question is one that signals the "right" answer: "Don't you find that [Competitor] is overpriced?" vs. "How would you describe the pricing of the tools you currently use?" The first question is useless. The second gives you real data.

Test before sending

Send the survey to 3–5 colleagues before distributing. Ask them to note anything confusing, ambiguous, or that felt like a trick question. Fix these before you send to your actual audience — you cannot correct a survey once responses are coming in.

How to Distribute Your Market Research Survey

Building a great market survey template is step one. Distributing it to the right people is step two — and the one most teams underinvest in.

Your own audience (highest quality)

Email your existing customers or trial users directly. Response rates from a warm list are 10–30x higher than cold outreach. Even 20 responses from real customers is more valuable than 200 from random internet users.

Community channels (faster, more diverse)

  • Reddit — post in subreddits where your target audience hangs out. Frame it as research, not promotion. Most communities allow genuine research posts if you explain what you're studying.
  • LinkedIn — share in relevant groups or on your personal feed if your network matches your target audience
  • Slack communities — industry-specific Slack groups often have a #feedback or #surveys channel
  • Product Hunt — if your product is live, your Ship page subscribers are a warm audience for research surveys

Incentivise participation

Even a small incentive (entry into a $50 gift card draw, a free month of your product, early access to results) can double your response rate. For B2B surveys where respondents are busy professionals, an incentive is often the difference between 5% and 20% completion.

Analysing Market Research Survey Results

Raw survey responses are inputs, not insights. Turning them into insights requires a process:

  1. Quantify the multiple choice — calculate percentages for each option. Look for answers that >40% of respondents chose — those are your strongest signals.
  2. Theme the open-ended text — read every open-ended response. Create 5–8 theme tags (e.g., "pricing concern", "missing feature X", "onboarding friction") and tag each response. Count which themes appear most.
  3. Quote the verbatim — for every theme, find the 2–3 responses that say it most clearly. These become copy for your landing page, ads, and sales emails.
  4. Segment by respondent type — if you collected role or company size, segment responses by those dimensions. The most valuable data is often when different segments disagree — that's where positioning choices live.
  5. Act on the top 3 findings — market research that doesn't produce action is just activity. Write a one-page summary with exactly three specific decisions the data informs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a market research survey be?

6–10 questions is the sweet spot for most market research goals. Under 5 questions rarely gives you enough data to work with. Over 10 questions significantly reduces completion rates — especially for cold audiences who have no prior relationship with your brand.

How many responses do I need for market research?

For qualitative insights (understanding language, pain points, themes), 20–30 responses is usually enough to identify patterns. For quantitative comparisons (is 60% or 40% of the market interested?), you need at least 100 responses for the percentages to be meaningful. For segmented analysis (comparing two different customer types), you need 30+ responses per segment.

Can I use the market survey template as a PDF?

Paper or PDF market survey templates work for in-person research (trade shows, workshops, interviews). For online distribution, a digital survey gives you automatic response collection, analytics, and easy export. The FormFlowX market research template is optimised for online distribution — mobile-responsive, multi-step to reduce overwhelm, and tracked so you can see exactly where respondents drop off.

What is the difference between a market survey and a market research survey?

"Market survey" and "market research survey" are often used interchangeably. The distinction, when one is made, is that a market survey is typically a broader snapshot of market size and pricing expectations, while a market research survey may include deeper questions about motivations, behaviour, and competitive landscape.
