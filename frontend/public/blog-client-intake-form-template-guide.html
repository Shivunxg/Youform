What Is a Client Intake Form?

A client intake form is a structured questionnaire you send to a new or prospective client before starting work together. It replaces the discovery call as the first step in onboarding — or makes that call ten times more productive by giving you real information to work from instead of starting cold.

Agencies, consultants, freelancers, coaches, and service businesses of every kind use client intake forms for the same reason: they turn scattered information gathering into a repeatable, professional process. Every new client answers the same core questions, in the same order, before anything else happens.

Why Every Service Business Needs One

Without a standard intake process, onboarding a new client usually looks like this: an introductory call to gather basic details, a follow-up email asking for the information you forgot to ask, another email chasing a late reply, and finally — a week or two in — you have enough to actually start. A client intake form template collapses that into one step.

  • Faster onboarding: Clients answer once, in their own time, instead of scheduling a call for information they could have typed in five minutes.
  • Better qualification: Budget and timeline questions upfront mean you stop discovering mismatches after you've already done the proposal.
  • Fewer mistakes: Structured fields (not a rambling email) mean nothing important gets missed or misread.
  • A more professional first impression: A clean, branded intake form signals you run an organised business — before the client has seen any deliverable.

Client Intake Form Best Practices

Group questions into sections

A wall of 15 unrelated questions feels heavier than the same 15 questions split into four labelled sections. Sectioning also lets you use conditional logic — for example, only showing "which platforms do you use?" to clients who said yes to "do you already have social media accounts?"

Keep required fields to the essentials

Mark only contact info and the core project question as required. Everything else — company website, secondary contacts, extra context — should be optional. Forms with fewer required fields have meaningfully higher completion rates.

Use conditional logic to shorten the form for each client

Not every client needs every question. A form builder like FormFlowX can route different follow-ups based on earlier answers — new clients see an extra "how did you hear about us?" question, returning clients skip straight to project scope.

Send it at the right moment

The best time to send a client intake form is immediately after a client agrees to move forward, before the discovery call — not after. That way the call itself is spent discussing their answers, not collecting them from scratch.

Connect it to your workflow

An intake form that just sits in a spreadsheet is only half useful. Set up a Slack or email notification for every new submission so you (or whoever owns onboarding) can respond same-day, while the client's interest is still highest.

Client Intake Form Templates by Industry

The core structure above works broadly, but a few industries benefit from extra fields:

  • Design and creative agencies: Add a brand guidelines upload field and a "style references" long-text question.
  • Marketing and SEO agencies: Add current tools/platforms in use and access-sharing instructions (Google Analytics, ad accounts).
  • Legal and financial consultants: Add a confidentiality acknowledgment and a document upload field.
  • Coaches and consultants: Add goals, current challenges, and availability for recurring sessions.
  • Web developers: Add hosting/domain details and existing tech stack questions.

Client Intake Form vs Onboarding Form: What's the Difference?

These two get confused often. A client intake form happens before you agree to work together — it qualifies the lead and scopes the project. An onboarding form happens after the contract is signed — it collects the operational details you need to actually deliver (logins, assets, contacts). Many businesses use FormFlowX for both, as two separate forms in the same workflow.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a client intake form include?

At minimum: contact details, a description of what the client needs, their budget range, and their timeline. Beyond that, tailor the form to your industry — add fields for brand assets, existing tools, or document uploads depending on what you need to start work.

Should I ask about budget on an intake form?

Yes. Asking budget range as a multiple-choice question (rather than an open text field) qualifies leads early and avoids wasted proposals. Clients are generally more comfortable picking a range than typing an exact number.

How long should a client intake form be?

Long enough to qualify and scope the project, short enough to finish in under five minutes — typically 8 to 12 questions. Use conditional logic to keep it short for any individual client, even if the full form covers more ground.
